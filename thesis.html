DESIGN THESIS / HOUSING STUDIO — 2026

Landscape as an Archive: Housing as Spatial Assemblage

Ocean Grove, Victoria, Australia

HERO IMAGE
PROJECT

Ocean Grove is currently experiencing an increased demand for housing, due to the appeal of coastal, slower-paced living. This demand is supported by the availability of land, particularly within expanding suburban edges such as Kingston Coast, resulting in widespread levelling of the landscape, removal of native vegetation, and the production of repetitive housing models that disregard ground conditions.

Built form can instead be conceived as an incremental spatial framework that accommodates density while sustaining the spatial and material memory embedded within the landscape.

By first responding to existing topography, ground conditions, and flora, this thesis presents housing as an adaptable model rather than a finished object. Patterns of inhabitation are encouraged to become legible in form, allowing dwelling to evolve alongside the ground rather than overwrite it.

RESEARCH QUESTION

In an environment where housing needs are increasing, but are damaging to the ground condition, how can built form be conceived as a framework that accommodates this demand while sustaining the spatial and material memory of the landscape, in turn allowing patterns of inhabitation to be expressed through form over time?

THE SITE

The project is located within Kingston Coast in Ocean Grove, positioned at the edge of a developing suburban housing estate and expansive farmland.

The site becomes a threshold between these two conditions, creating opportunities to reconsider density, ecological corridors, productive landscapes and shared spaces.

LANDSCAPE

Ocean Grove's landscape condition is defined by a fragile coastal ecology of sand dunes, low coastal scrub, wetlands and wind-shaped topographies.

Rather than treating the landscape as a neutral surface on which housing is placed, the project considers the ground as an active archive containing ecological, spatial and material memory.

THE COAST

Rock formations around the Surf Coast became an important conceptual reference for the project. Subtraction and erosion demonstrate how time and natural forces can produce cavities, thresholds and inhabitable spaces.

This idea of carving space through subtraction informs the spatial organisation of the proposal, where voids and thresholds become places capable of being shaped by their inhabitants.

RESEARCH

The project draws upon research into adaptable housing, user participation, thresholds and changing patterns of occupation.

Key precedents include N. John Habraken's Supports and Infill, Stewart Brand's Shearing Layers of Change, and Aldo van Eyck's theories of interstitial space and the in-between.

CONCEPT

Housing is conceived not as a finished object, but as a framework capable of changing with its inhabitants and its landscape.

The project explores the removal of conventional internal corridors and their replacement with external thresholds, decks and shared spaces. These become opportunities for circulation, interaction and inhabitation.

DESIGN DEVELOPMENT

The proposal develops a cluster housing model in which individual dwellings are arranged around shared landscape and productive garden spaces.

Dwellings are organised through a series of structural planes that separate living and sleeping spaces while allowing the houses to grow as requirements change.

THE PROPOSAL

The final proposal concentrates housing into clusters, allowing portions of the site to remain open and ecologically productive.

Shared decks, gardens and thresholds form a collective landscape between dwellings, creating spaces that sit between private occupation and communal life.
