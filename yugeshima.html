Project

Architecture as a living archive.

Akiya are vacant homes scattered throughout Japan as a consequence of depopulation and the changing conditions of rural communities. This project asks how an existing house can be revitalised without erasing the history embedded within it.

Located on Yugeshima in Kamijima, Ehime, the project transforms an existing akiya, Nagi's House, into a creative residence for artists, makers and designers.

Rather than treating preservation as an attempt to freeze the building in time, the proposal considers the house as a living archive — a framework that can continue to change through inhabitation.

03 / MATERIAL HISTORY

Materials
remember.

Materials change through occupation. Timber becomes polished, darkened and marked through repeated touch, while steel records environmental exposure through oxidation, weathering and corrosion. These transformations allow material itself to become a record of time.

Weathered Timber

Timber

Existing timber is retained wherever possible. Scratches, dents, discolouration and wear are not treated simply as defects, but as evidence of occupation.

The existing structure therefore becomes part of the architectural language of the proposal, allowing the history of the house to remain visible.

Steel

New steel provides a contrasting layer of intervention. Unlike the timber, which records human touch, steel records environmental conditions and provides a durable structural framework.

The distinction between old and new is deliberately legible, allowing the intervention to communicate the ongoing transformation of the building.

Steel Reinforcement

05 / SPATIAL ADAPTABILITY

A house that
can change.

The creative residence is designed to accommodate different residents and creative disciplines. Rather than assigning a permanent function to every space, adaptable elements allow occupants to modify the building according to their needs.

Shoji + Fusuma Adaptability

Flexible thresholds

Traditional Japanese elements such as shoji and fusuma provide a precedent for spaces that can open, close, divide and combine.

This principle is translated into the creative residence, where spaces can shift between individual work, collaborative activity, exhibition and community use.

Flexible Studio Configuration

07 / SCAFFOLDING

Architecture
as a kit.

Unused steel scaffolding sourced from shipbuilding sites around Kamijima becomes a temporary construction system within the courtyard. Residents can assemble, modify and dismantle elements according to their needs.

Scaffolding Intervention

Temporary / permanent

The scaffolding introduces a layer of impermanence into the architecture. It can become storage, shelter, display space, outdoor working space or a structure for hanging materials.

The system allows the residents themselves to participate in the continual construction of the building.

12 / REFLECTION

Building through
time.

The project explores preservation not as an attempt to return an existing building to a fixed historical state, but as a means of allowing it to continue developing.

Through the combination of aged timber, new steel reinforcement and temporary scaffolding, Nagi's House becomes a framework for continued inhabitation. Its future history is not predetermined; it is created through the people who use it.
