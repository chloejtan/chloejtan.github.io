01 / RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

Architecture as a
living archive.

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

A building and its materials hold and reflect the interactions that the space has had with people and its surroundings.

The research proposal is centred on an akiya on Yugeshima, and seeks to address depopulation and the post-growth community condition in Kamijima by embracing adaptability and the ability for a building to provide multiple affordances to creatives.


The creative residence explores the potential of architecture as a living archive, continuously shaped by the interactions and gestures of its inhabitants.

Research Question

How can a building be structurally reinforced to extend its lifespan whilst retaining the history and memories tied to it, creating a framework for the revitalisation of rural communities around Japan?

03 / RESEARCH

Post-growth
& depopulation

KAMIJIMA DEMOGRAPHICS

Kamijima

Kamijima is facing depopulation and the effects of a post-growth society. Younger generations increasingly migrate to major cities for education and employment, leaving rural towns with aging populations and vacant homes.

KAMIJIMA / YUGESHIMA

Rural renewal

Rather than viewing population decline as the end of a community, the project reframes the condition as an opportunity for renewal, where architecture is continuously shaped by the people who engage with it.

04 / AKIYA

空き家
Akiya

AKIYA PHOTOGRAPH
Fig. 7. A vacant house on Yugeshima. Photograph by Author, 2025.

The vacant house as opportunity.

Akiya are vacant homes that have become widespread throughout rural Japan as a result of depopulation and social change.

Rather than treating these buildings as redundant, the proposal considers the existing structure as a repository of material and cultural memory.

The intervention therefore seeks to extend the life of the building without erasing the traces that make it meaningful.

08 / 02

Preservation
& reinforcement

Old timber is retained as a record of the building's history while new steel provides structural longevity.

Old + new.

Worn timber, marked by human use, tells the story of the akiya's past life.

New steel provides structural reinforcement and a fresh beginning, creating a dialogue between old and new materials.

Steel sections such as equal angles, PFCs and universal columns can cap and reinforce existing timber members while allowing the original structure to remain visible.

STEEL + TIMBER DETAIL
Steel reinforcement intervention.
09 / SHIP OF THESEUS

What remains when everything changes?

SHIP OF THESEUS DIAGRAM

Continuity through change.

The Ship of Theseus paradox questions whether an object remains the same when all of its parts are gradually replaced.

This mirrors traditional Japanese construction, where buildings can be repaired and renewed while retaining their identity through continued use, form and memory.

Chidori Bunka transforms a disused industrial building into an evolving creative community space. Salvaged materials are redistributed throughout the project and artists are encouraged to construct their own workspaces.

14 / SCAFFOLDING

Temporary
architecture.

A flexible scaffolding system allows residents to construct their own temporary spaces.

DIY + impermanence.

The courtyard provides an opportunity for creative residents to customise their working environment.

A contemporary artist may require a sheltered outdoor area for spray painting, while a seamstress may need temporary space to hang fabric.

The scaffolding becomes an architectural framework rather than a predetermined building.
