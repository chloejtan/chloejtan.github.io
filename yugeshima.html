Yugeshima Creative Residence Nagi's House

Yugeshima, Kamijima, Ehime, Japan

Project

A house is a vessel for memories, experiences and exchange, providing inhabitants with a space to commune and gather, building communities and facilitating activities and discussion.

Located on Yugeshima in the Kamijima region of Ehime, Japan, the project explores the revitalisation of an existing akiya through adaptive reuse, structural reinforcement and spatial adaptability. The house is transformed into a creative residence and social hub for artists, makers and designers.

The project asks how a building can be structurally reinforced to extend its lifespan whilst retaining the history and memories tied to it, creating a framework for the revitalisation of rural communities around Japan.

Existing timber is retained as a record of the building's past, while new steel interventions provide reinforcement and a framework for future inhabitation. The contrast between aged timber and new steel makes the process of repair and renewal visible within the architecture.

Hero Image — Nagi's House

Overview

The project responds to the effects of depopulation and post-growth conditions affecting rural communities throughout Japan. As younger generations migrate towards major cities, rural towns are left with vacant homes, underutilised spaces and ageing communities.

Rather than viewing these conditions as the end of a community, the project reframes the akiya as an opportunity for renewal. Nagi's House becomes a creative residence where architecture can continuously change through the people who inhabit and interact with it.

The proposal combines traditional Japanese construction principles with contemporary interventions. Timber joinery, shoji, fusuma, tatami and the engawa inform the spatial organisation, while steel reinforcement and temporary scaffolding provide a contemporary framework for adaptation.

Research

The research investigates the relationship between material, memory and time. Materials are understood as vessels of memory, carrying physical traces of human occupation, environmental change and repair.

Timber records human interaction through wear, polishing, discolouration, dents and scratches. Steel, by contrast, records environmental exposure through oxidation, weathering and corrosion. Together, the two materials establish a dialogue between human memory and environmental memory.

The Ship of Theseus paradox further informed the proposal, questioning whether a building remains the same building as its individual components are gradually repaired and replaced. This idea became central to the approach of preserving Nagi's House while allowing it to change over time.

Design

The proposal introduces a creative residence within Nagi's House, combining public, communal and private spaces. The ground floor contains a gallery, cafe, flexible studio and garden, while the upper floor provides short-term accommodation and shared domestic spaces.

The studio is intentionally designed without a fixed creative programme. Instead, spaces are defined through adaptable architectural elements that allow residents to modify their environment according to their discipline and needs.

Shoji and fusuma provide movable thresholds between spaces, allowing rooms to expand and contract according to different activities. This allows the building to accommodate painters, sculptors, potters, furniture designers, filmmakers and other creative residents.

Adaptability

Adaptability extends beyond the interior of the residence. The courtyard becomes a temporary creative landscape where residents can construct their own spaces using a reusable scaffolding system.

The scaffolding is intended to be sourced from unused steel associated with shipbuilding sites around Kamijima. Its temporary nature allows residents to construct shelters, storage, work surfaces, hanging systems and other structures according to their individual needs.

The intervention creates a distinction between permanent and temporary architecture. The existing house provides the permanent framework, while the scaffolding allows inhabitants to continually modify and personalise the spaces around it.

Structural Intervention

The existing timber structure is retained wherever possible, allowing the original material and construction system to remain visible. Where timber has become structurally compromised, steel sections are introduced to reinforce the existing framework.

Steel angles, PFCs, universal columns and other steel sections are used to cap and reinforce aged timber beams and columns. The intervention provides additional structural capacity while maintaining the historical presence of the original timber.

Red is used to identify the new intervention. It distinguishes repair and reinforcement from the existing building, creating a visible dialogue between old and new.

Model

Physical models were used to test the structural and spatial ideas developed through the research. Three scales were used to investigate different aspects of the proposal: 1:1, 1:2 and 1:20.

The 1:1 models explored the temporary scaffolding system and its connection details. The 1:2 models tested steel reinforcement strategies for aged timber, while the 1:20 model explored the overall structural arrangement of Nagi's House.

1:2 Intervention Models

The 1:2 models explore the relationship between aged timber and new steel reinforcement. The prototypes test how new structural elements can support the existing material while keeping the original timber legible.

The fabrication process also investigated material sourcing, 3D printing, tolerance and assembly, allowing the structural details to be refined through physical testing.

1:20 Model

The 1:20 structural model illustrates the overall arrangement of posts, beams and roof structure within Nagi's House. It highlights the locations where steel reinforcement is introduced into the existing timber framework.

The model demonstrates how new structural supports can be integrated without erasing the original construction system, reinforcing the project's approach to memory and adaptive reuse.

1:20 Structural Model

Final Deliverables

The final proposal brings together the research into material memory, structural reinforcement and spatial adaptability. Nagi's House is retained as a living framework rather than treated as a static artefact.

Through the combination of aged timber, new steel reinforcement, adaptable interior partitions and temporary scaffolding, the creative residence is designed to evolve alongside its inhabitants and the wider community of Yugeshima.
