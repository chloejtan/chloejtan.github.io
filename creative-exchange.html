A House for Creative Exchange

Sint-Jans-Molenbeek, Brussels, Belgium

Project

A house is a space to commune, to share and collaborate, a facilitator for exchange, perhaps one that evolves through time by cause of the inhabitants.

Located in a multi-faceted community in the Sint-Jans-Molenbeek area of Brussels, the house will be a creative residence and studio space for artists of all natures, a threshold connecting the community whilst providing affordance for individuals to convene for events and exhibitions to be held.

Thresholds are not only indicators of space and programme, delineating characteristics of the two, but also connections between movement and dwelling, practices and communities. In this language of thresholds as a connective tissue, an emphasis is placed on small architectural nuances and elements, creating new spaces; an unused space can be transformed through the introduction of a singular linear threshold.

Currently a petrol station, demolished elements within the existing building will be reused through adaptive reuse techniques, and as the space evolves and experiences use, the architecture is intended to evolve through influence of its inhabitants.

Overview

The project proposes the adaptive reuse of an existing petrol station in Brussels as a creative residence and studio for artists. The building establishes a series of thresholds between the street, public exhibition spaces, shared working spaces and private residences.

The project was developed through an iterative process of surveying, model making, drawing and testing material and spatial relationships.

Site / Context Image

Process

The project began with an extensive survey of the existing building and its surrounding urban context. Physical models, facade studies and structural investigations were used to understand the existing conditions before developing the proposal.

Iterative model making became an important part of the design process, allowing different relationships between the building, street and public realm to be tested at a range of scales.

Design

The final proposal establishes an open studio and exhibition space on the ground floor, artist residences above, and a rooftop terrace incorporating a cafe and urban farming area.

A series of architectural thresholds connect these different programmes. The ground floor opens towards the street through large arched openings and integrated seating, creating a gradual transition between the public realm and the interior.

A central atrium provides a vertical connection through the building, allowing light to penetrate the interior while creating a shared space for exhibition and interaction.
