A House for
Creative Exchange

Hero image

Project

A house is a space to commune, to share and collaborate, a facilitator for exchange, perhaps one that evolves through time by cause of the inhabitants.

Located in a multi-faceted community in the Sint-Jans-Molenbeek area of Brussels, the house will be a creative residence and studio space for artists of all natures, a threshold connecting the community whilst providing affordance for individuals to convene for events and exhibitions to be held.

Thresholds are not only indicators of space and programme, delineating characteristics of the two, but also connections between movement and dwelling, practices and communities. In this language of thresholds as a connective tissue, an emphasis is placed on small architectural nuances and elements, creating new spaces; an unused space can be transformed through the introduction of a singular linear threshold.

Currently a petrol station, demolished elements within the existing building will be reused through adaptive reuse techniques, and as the space evolves and experiences use, the architecture is intended to evolve through influence of its inhabitants.

Site

Brussels, Belgium

The project is located within the Sint-Jans-Molenbeek area of Brussels, at the intersection of Hopstraat and Boulevard Barthélemy. The existing site is a petrol station, situated within a diverse urban and social context.

Initial site investigations focused on understanding the relationship between the existing building, the street, surrounding buildings and the wider community.

Concept

Thresholds as connective tissue

The project explores thresholds as more than boundaries between spaces. They become connective elements between movement and dwelling, public and private space, individual practice and collective activity.

Through this understanding, small architectural interventions are used to create new relationships between the building, its inhabitants and the surrounding community.

Programme

A creative residence

The building is conceived as a creative residence and studio for artists, providing spaces for making, living, exhibiting and gathering.

The final programme consists of:

  • Ground Floor — Open Studio and Exhibition Space
  • First Floor — Artist Residences
  • Rooftop — Cafe and Urban Farming

The building is designed to accommodate both individual creative practice and collective events, allowing the programme to shift according to the needs of its inhabitants.

Development

Iterations

The design developed through a series of physical models, drawings and iterations. Each iteration tested the relationship between the existing building, the street and the new programme.

Early proposals explored setbacks, changes in level and openings as ways of gradually transitioning between the public street and the interior of the building.

Final Proposal

Carved thresholds

The final iteration developed the idea of the bay window and transformed it into a carved threshold within the street facade. The intervention creates a moment of pause and occupation, allowing seating to extend towards the footpath and activate the street.

A precast concrete lattice surrounds the rooftop terrace, filtering sunlight while allowing plants and an urban ecosystem to develop. The same material language extends into the stairwell, where blue tiles connect the intervention to the neighbouring building.
